Guide · governance & comparability

Related-party DAT adviser agreements: benchmark the stack, not the label.

A disclosed related-party relationship can make a provider agreement more important to review, but the label alone does not tell a Board whether the economics are high, low, comparable or justified. The work starts by separating objective relationship facts from economic and governance terms.

1. Identify the overlap precisely

“Related party” can describe different facts: provider ownership by a director or executive, overlapping control, strategic investors, adviser principals serving on the Board, financing-linked relationships or other disclosed affiliations. The source record should say exactly what the overlap is instead of using the label as a conclusion.

Relationship fact ≠ pricing conclusion. The benchmark still needs independent comparables with similar role, scope, term and compensation architecture.

2. Pull governance rights into the same table as fees

A provider may receive more than money. Public agreements can include consent, consultation, nomination or other governance rights that affect the company's ability to change managers, amend investment guidelines or make leadership decisions. Those rights are part of the economic and control profile even when they do not have an obvious dollar price.

Governance fields worth normalizing

  • Board seat or nomination rights
  • Chairman designation or other leadership rights
  • Consent rights over manager changes or amendments
  • Consultation rights on executive changes
  • Investment-guideline approval rights
  • Information and access rights
  • Related financing or capital commitments
  • Ownership or control relationships
  • Committee review / approval process disclosed by the issuer

3. Treat equity economics as compensation, not decoration

Shares, preferred stock and warrants can materially change the provider's economics and incentives. A clean comparison should record the grant date, number of securities, exercise price, vesting or forfeiture conditions, financing-linked formulas and whether rights survive termination.

It should also version the state of an equity-linked compensation right. Hyperliquid Strategies / Rorschach is a useful public control: the executed adviser agreement contains a future-financing formula of shares equal to 5% of the qualifying financing share count plus warrants equal to 15%, while a later 2026 filing says that right is waived unless the adviser revokes the waiver for future financings on two weeks' advance notice. That is not the same as deleting the formula from the contract.

Compensation-state rule: contractual right · current waiver / suspension · revocation mechanics · as-of date should be stored separately.

4. Separate transaction equity, service compensation and governance rights

The same provider can receive securities at transaction closing, have a separate future service-compensation formula and hold governance rights under another agreement. Those layers should not be blended into one “provider fee.” Hyperliquid Strategies illustrates the distinction: Rorschach's closing shares and warrants are a separate economic layer from the future-financing grant formula, while its Advisor Rights Agreement separately addresses conditional Board nomination and information rights.

5. Related-party review needs independent comparables

The strongest peer set is not simply “other related-party DAT companies.” It is a set of agreements that are economically comparable: similar provider role, asset mandate, discretion, term, fee base, performance mechanics and exit structure. Related-party status is then an additional governance dimension.

6. Currentness matters because relationships change

Provider ownership, Board composition, finance leadership, compensation status and contract terms can all change after the original agreement is signed. A benchmark should use current filings and amendments rather than assume that the original relationship map still governs. A waiver, fee pause or non-renewal can materially change current economics even when the underlying treasury strategy continues.

7. Board-side questions to answer

  • What exactly makes the provider related to the issuer?
  • What services and decision authority does the provider actually have?
  • How do recurring, performance and equity economics compare with independent mandates?
  • Are any compensation rights currently waived, paused or capable of being reactivated?
  • Which rights could affect amendment, replacement or exit?
  • Which Board nomination, Chairman, information or consent rights sit in a separate agreement family?
  • Were material terms amended after the original approval?
  • What realized public outcomes exist for comparable arrangements?
  • Which facts support comparability and which facts make a peer non-comparable?

8. Keep factual benchmarking separate from legal conclusions

A public-source stress test can identify disclosed relationships, compare economics, map governance rights and show public precedents. It should not convert those facts into an opinion that directors satisfied or breached fiduciary duties, that a transaction was fair or unfair, or that a contract is enforceable.

The research question is: “What does the public record show, and how do the economics and control rights compare?” — not “Who is legally right?”
