Axe Compute / DNA: economics are only half the currentness problem.

The Axe Compute / DNA Holdings agreement family illustrates a different stress-test risk: a large related-party compensation stack can remain economically important while the people around the relationship and the issuer's public disclosure evolve. Current contract status therefore has to be verified across filing periods, not inferred from an old agreement, a personnel change or silence in one later filing.

Public facts

October 7, 2025

Strategic-advisor and asset-management agreements

The company engaged DNA Holdings Venture as strategic advisor and digital-asset manager. Public filings describe a 10-year asset-management agreement with a 1.00% annual asset-based fee plus a 25% incentive fee on profits above 7%.

2025 compensation

Separate equity economics

The company granted DNA strategic-advisor warrants with a disclosed grant-date fair value of approximately $15.4 million. It also disclosed approximately $722,390 of asset-management fees paid to DNA during 2025.

Related-party structure at launch

Provider personnel entered issuer governance

Shawn Matthews, then DNA's CEO, joined the issuer's Board. Thomas McLaughlin, then DNA's CIO, became the issuer's Chief Investment Officer. The company disclosed the DNA arrangements in its related-party note, and its Audit Committee policy requires review and approval of transactions that must be disclosed under Item 404(a) of Regulation S-K.

January–February 2026

Relationship map changed

Matthews resigned from the Board and McLaughlin resigned as Chief Investment Officer in January. In February, Axe appointed Christopher Miglino as CEO; the appointment disclosure states that Miglino had served as President and Co-Founder of DNA from January 2024 to January 2026 and separately references DNA's warrant economics from the ATH transaction.

March 31, 2026 reporting

The agreement was still being described as current

The company's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 again states that DNA will act as the company's digital-asset manager under the 10-year Asset Management Agreement and repeats the 1.00% annual fee plus the 25% incentive fee above 7%.

June 30, 2026 reporting

Relationship disclosure disappears while the treasury strategy remains active

The June 2026 Form 10-Q continues to describe an active ATH treasury strategy, approximately $11.1 million of ATH held at quarter-end, a digital-asset lending transaction and the Compute Services and Treasury Management segment. But searchable disclosure in that filing no longer repeats DNA, the Asset Management Agreement or the Strategic Advisor Agreement. That change does not by itself establish termination.

Stress-test lesson

A useful DAT provider record needs two separate tracks: contract currentness and relationship currentness. In this case, a March filing expressly repeated the manager agreement after the January personnel departures, while the June filing kept the treasury strategy but dropped the DNA agreement language. The correct output is therefore an unresolved current-status question unless an amendment, termination or later affirmative disclosure closes the gap.

Observable economics from public filings

  • 10-year asset-management term.
  • 1.00% annual asset-based management fee.
  • 25% incentive fee on profits above 7%.
  • $15.4 million disclosed grant-date fair value of strategic-advisor warrants.
  • $722,390 of asset-management fees disclosed for 2025.

What this case note does not say

  • It does not conclude that the agreement is currently active or terminated.
  • It does not conclude that the economics are fair or unfair.
  • It does not infer misconduct from former or current personnel relationships.
  • It shows why currentness, conflicts, compensation and filing-to-filing disclosure need separate verification.
