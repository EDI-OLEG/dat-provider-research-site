Case note · Axe Compute / DNA Holdings Venture

A 1% management fee can sit beside a 25% incentive fee and a ten-year mandate with no ordinary client exit path.

Axe Compute, formerly Predictive Oncology, provides a useful discretionary-manager control because its DNA agreement combines recurring and incentive economics, broad operating authority, related-party governance facts and an unusually asymmetric termination structure. Later filings then create a separate currentness question when the treasury strategy remains visible but the DNA agreement language disappears.

The exit asymmetry is the core comparison field

The executed Asset Management Agreement gives the client a termination right for specified Asset Manager Cause Events, subject to notice and cure mechanics. By contrast, beginning after the first anniversary the manager may terminate without a Client Cause Event on 90 days' notice. The public contract therefore does not present a symmetrical ordinary no-cause exit path for both sides.

Normalization rule: do not treat “10-year term” as enough information. Store client ordinary exit, client cause exit, manager ordinary exit and manager cause exit as separate fields.

The percentage also understates the compensation stack

DNA's recurring economics are not just a 1% AUM-style charge. The filed structure adds a 25% incentive fee on profits above 7%, documented account expenses and separate strategic-advisor warrants. Axe's 2025 Form 10-K disclosed approximately $722,390 of asset-management fees paid to DNA during 2025 and approximately $15.4 million grant-date fair value for the strategic-advisor warrants.

The provider relationship was related-party at launch

Public filings state that Shawn Matthews, then DNA's CEO, joined the issuer's Board and Thomas McLaughlin, then DNA's CIO, became the issuer's Chief Investment Officer. Those personnel later left those issuer roles, and Axe subsequently appointed Christopher Miglino, who had served as President and Co-Founder of DNA through January 2026, as Chief Executive Officer. These are relationship facts rather than a conclusion about fairness or pricing.

March 2026 still described DNA as the manager

The March 31, 2026 Form 10-Q continued to describe an active ATH treasury strategy and repeated that DNA managed it under the ten-year Asset Management Agreement. That filing again disclosed the 1.00% annual asset-based fee and 25% incentive fee above 7% after the earlier personnel changes.

June 2026 creates a currentness gap, not proof of termination

The June 30, 2026 Form 10-Q continued to report digital assets, losses on digital assets and a treasury-management operating history, while the business narrative had shifted strongly toward GPU compute infrastructure. Searchable text in that later filing no longer repeats DNA, the Asset Management Agreement or the Strategic Advisor Agreement.

Silence in a later filing does not establish termination. No amendment or termination document is identified here that closes the agreement family. The appropriate status for a public-source stress test is therefore currentness unresolved: March provides affirmative manager evidence; June preserves the treasury context but no longer affirmatively states the manager relationship.

Why this case matters for a Board / CFO stress test

Axe adds a clean control for four separate problems: base fee versus incentive compensation; asymmetric client / manager exit rights; related-party and personnel overlap; and a later disclosure gap after a business pivot. A fee table alone would miss all four.
