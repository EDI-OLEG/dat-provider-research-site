Case note · AlphaTON / DWF

AlphaTON / DWF: a provider-funded TON mandate that moved from launch terms to settlement in three months.

AlphaTON Capital's DWF MaaS Treasury Management Agreement is a useful example of why digital asset treasury provider economics may combine asset management, financing, equity compensation and investor repurchase rights in the same transaction family.

Original treasury management structure

AlphaTON entered into a Treasury Management Agreement with DWF MaaS Limited dated August 25, 2025 as part of the launch of its TON treasury strategy. Later filings describe DWF as contributing $15 million in the September 2025 financing and managing that capital for the TON strategy.

The financing and management economics were intertwined

The DWF relationship was not a simple recurring management-fee mandate. DWF was simultaneously a financing participant, a treasury manager and a recipient of restricted shares. The agreement family also included a right for DWF to convert securities subscribed for in the financing into an equivalent value of TON from assets held on behalf of the company, up to $15 million, during a defined period.

Benchmarking implication: provider economics should not be reduced to a percentage fee when financing capital, equity compensation, managed assets and repurchase / conversion rights are part of the same transaction family.

The planned scale-up did not occur before settlement

AlphaTON later disclosed that through December 30, 2025 there had been no additional transfers to DWF and DWF was managing only the $15 million that it had contributed in the September financing.

December 2025 settlement terminated the agreement

On December 30, 2025, AlphaTON and DWF entered into a Settlement Agreement and Release resolving disputes over rights and obligations under the Treasury Management Agreement. AlphaTON agreed to purchase the relevant DWF securities for $15 million, or the amount remaining in the digital wallet used to fund the original deposit; remove vesting restrictions on the 160,000 restricted shares; and pay DWF $35,000. The parties granted mutual releases, and the Treasury Management Agreement and Restricted Share Agreement were terminated.

Later annual-report disclosure states that the DWF pre-funded warrants were forfeited in connection with return of DWF's initial investment and that AlphaTON recognized treasury stock when DWF's returned shares came back to the company.

What this case contributes to a DAT provider benchmark

AlphaTON / DWF shows that a provider agreement can be inseparable from the financing structure that launched the treasury strategy. It also shows why a decision-grade currentness record must follow the transaction past the original agreement: within months, the planned transfer schedule had not been completed and the relationship moved into a settlement and full release.
