What gets stress-tested

Provider economics

  • Recurring AUM / advisory fee and denominator

Why this matters

The public set already spans 0.6% to 1.75% recurring manager economics, one- to twenty-year initial structures, cause-based exits, short renewal cycles and realized termination disputes. A blended “market rate” would hide the decision-relevant differences.

Why contract-stack analysis matters

The expensive part may not be the headline fee.

Lock-in can dominate economics

A recurring fee that looks comparable can sit inside a very different term and termination structure. Public DAT agreements include long-duration arrangements where company-side termination can expose the issuer to substantial remaining-term economics.

Real outcomes change the benchmark

Public issuers have already renegotiated fee architecture, internalized provider functions, negotiated exits and litigated termination provisions. Those realized outcomes are more useful than a fee table alone.

Method

We do not treat headline percentages as a market rate. A 1% strategic-advisory arrangement is not automatically comparable to a 1% discretionary asset-management mandate.

TARGET → CURRENT CONTRACT VERSION → ROLE/SCOPE → FEE & DENOMINATOR → TERM/RENEWAL → TERMINATION/EXIT MATH → EQUITY/OTHER CONSIDERATION → CONFLICTS/GOVERNANCE → REALIZED OUTCOMES → INTERNALIZATION/REPLACEMENT → TRANSITION CONTROLS → SOURCE LEDGER

Two-pass source process

Screen: current 8-K, 10-Q, 10-K and proxy disclosures.
Verify: executed agreement, amendments / restatements, current-period filing, governance disclosures and any public outcome record.

Version control

Every contract record is tracked as ORIGINAL → AMENDED → CURRENT → TERMINATED? so a superseded agreement is not benchmarked as if it were current.

Public outcome signals

The public record already contains materially different provider lifecycle outcomes. These are factual comparators, not opinions on legality, fairness or fiduciary compliance.

  • AVAX One / Hivemind: a 1.25% related-party discretionary manager relationship with a 10-year term and approximately $1.0 million of first-half 2026 fees.
  • Upexi / GSR: a 20-year, 1.75% arrangement later terminated with arbitration active in 2026 disclosures.
  • USBC: provider compensation was reset from recurring AUM + performance economics to a performance-only structure with a high-water mark and reconciliation / clawback mechanics.
  • SharpLink: the company terminated an external manager arrangement and internalized core treasury-management functions without an early-termination penalty in the disclosed outcome.
  • CleanCore: a provider unwind involved a large digital-asset transfer followed by restatement / material-weakness disclosures and additional termination / transfer controls.
