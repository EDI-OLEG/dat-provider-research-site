Currentness changes the answer

CEA / 10X shows why a historical benchmark can become stale while the contract remains active: the latest public filing describes a 1.4% current fee, unpaid accruals and ongoing litigation after an earlier Board benchmark referenced 1.75%.

Realized exits can differ from contract formulas

Forum Markets disclosed an 85% remaining-compensation formula, then later negotiated a $1.5M termination payment. Contractual exposure and realized exit consideration belong in separate fields.

Method

We do not treat headline percentages as a market rate. A planned agreement is not an executed agreement, and a 1.25% strategic-advisory fee based on issuer market capitalization is not automatically comparable to a 1.25% discretionary asset-management fee based on Account Size.

TARGET → AGREEMENT FAMILY → PLANNED / SIGNED / ACTIVE STATE → ROLE / AUTHORITY → FEE & DENOMINATOR → PAYMENT MEDIUM → EFFECTIVE VS OBSERVED → TERM / RENEWAL → TERMINATION / EXIT MATH → EQUITY / OTHER CONSIDERATION → GOVERNANCE → REALIZED OUTCOMES → SOURCE LEDGER

Public outcome signals

The public record already contains materially different provider lifecycle outcomes. These are factual comparators, not opinions on legality, fairness or fiduciary compliance.

  • CEA / 10X: an earlier Board benchmark referenced 1.75%; the latest filing describes 1.4%, $1.4M accrued unpaid fees and continuing litigation.
  • Hivemind: AVAX One and Upexi both show a 1.25% headline rate, but one is a 10-year discretionary Account Size manager mandate and the other a three-year market-cap-based non-discretionary advisory agreement paid in shares.
  • GSR: Lite Strategy and former Upexi mandates both show 1.75%, but payment medium, separate equity, exclusivity, duration, exit math and realized outcome differ materially.
  • Forum Markets: a 2% fee plus $2M minimum and 85% exit formula later ended in a negotiated $1.5M payment.
  • SharpLink: external management was internalized without an early-termination penalty in the disclosed outcome.
