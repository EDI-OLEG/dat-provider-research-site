Case note · Pineapple Financial / Canary + Monarq

Two 1% fee schedules, a 30/70 manager split and a currentness question.

Pineapple Financial's INJ treasury structure is a useful control for provider-stack analysis because two separate discretionary agreements sit around one treasury program. The headline rates look similar, but the allocation, authority, withdrawal mechanics, term and later disclosure trail are not interchangeable.

The two 1% rates should not be added together

The filed Canary agreement assigns 30% of Pineapple's Available Capital to Canary and the remaining 70% to Monarq. Each provider has a separate 1% annualized fee architecture, but those percentages apply to different allocated sleeves / account definitions. Treating the stack as a simple 2% treasury fee would therefore misstate the disclosed structure.

Normalization rule: provider fee rate × provider-specific denominator × allocated sleeve. Similar percentages across parallel providers are not automatically cumulative over the same asset base.

Canary adds allocation and withdrawal controls

The Canary agreement goes beyond a fee schedule. It limits ordinary withdrawals to one per calendar quarter and generally to no more than $10,000 of INJ. After the first anniversary, a larger withdrawal for legitimate business purposes requires approval from the independent directors. A withdrawal that would remove more than 50.1% of the total Account value also requires approval from holders of at least 50.1% of the company's voting power.

The agreement also states that Pineapple may not appoint another provider to perform the same or substantially similar services for Canary's 30% allocation without Canary's prior written consent, which the manager may withhold in its sole discretion. That restriction does not apply to capital outside Canary's allocated sleeve.

Currentness needs provider-by-provider evidence

The 2025 agreements were linked to escrow-release conditions around the INJ treasury financing. By 2026 the strategy was plainly funded and operational: Pineapple reported approximately $49.4 million of recognized digital assets at May 31, 2026 and continued to acquire INJ after quarter-end.

The same May 31, 2026 Form 10-Q specifically identified Monarq Capital as a digital-asset manager and disclosed $250,000 of restricted cash held by Monarq for future INJ purchases. The filing's counterparty-risk discussion also names Monarq alongside FalconX and BitGo.

That filing does not contain the name Canary Capital. Silence is not evidence of termination. The filed Canary agreement had a three-year initial term and cause-based early termination mechanics, so the public record should be treated as a provider-specific currentness question: Monarq has direct later-period operating evidence; Canary's later status requires separate confirmation rather than assumption.

Why this case improves a Board / CFO stress test

A one-line market table would likely record "1%" and miss most of the decision-relevant structure. Pineapple shows why a provider benchmark should separately store allocation percentage, denominator, billing timing, withdrawal controls, replacement-consent rights, term mechanics and later-period evidence for each provider in a multi-manager stack.

Decision-record fields this case adds

Provider-specific sleeve allocation · fee denominator · quarterly billing convention · large-withdrawal approval mechanics · replacement-provider consent rights · funded-status evidence · provider-by-provider currentness.
