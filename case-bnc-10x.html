Case note · current through Sep. 11, 2026

CEA / 10X: benchmark → fee reset → non-payment → live litigation.

CEA Industries is one of the clearest public examples of why DAT agreement benchmarking must track currentness rather than freeze the first disclosed fee. The Board publicly benchmarked a 20-year 10X Capital mandate in early 2026. By the September 2026 quarterly filing, the company described the live AMA as a flat 1.4% fee, disclosed $1.1 million of quarterly management expense, $1.4 million of accrued unpaid fees, and continuing litigation over the agreement and its termination provision.

Currentness finding: the 1.75% number used in CEA's March 2026 Market Proposal is historically important, but it is not the latest disclosed current fee. The September 2026 10-Q describes the AMA as a flat 1.4% fee. A benchmark that copied the March press release without checking the latest periodic filing would already be stale.

Public timeline

August 2025

Original long-duration AMA

CEA entered into the asset-management relationship with 10X for its BNB-focused treasury strategy. The agreement was structured as a long-duration mandate with management fees, warrants and material termination economics.

February–March 2026

Board sought market-standard amendments

CEA disclosed that its Board and Strategic Committee benchmarked the agreement and delivered a Market Proposal. The March 26 disclosure described the then-existing management fee as 1.75% of treasury-asset NAV and proposed 0.50% plus a possible performance bonus of up to 0.25%, a two-year term and revised termination economics.

May 22, 2026

Dispute moved into litigation

CEA sued in the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware seeking to void the AMA from inception and recover fees, or alternatively to invalidate the liquidated-damages provision.

July 28 / August 14, 2026

Dismissal motion and opposition

The Asset Manager moved to dismiss on July 28. CEA filed its opposition on August 14. The motion remained pending in the September 11 filing.

September 11, 2026

Latest filing turns the contract dispute into a live accrued-liability problem

The quarterly filing describes a flat 1.4% fee through 2045, $1.1 million of management expense for the quarter, and $1.4 million of accrued unpaid asset-management fees at July 31. CEA also says it has not paid invoiced management fees since April 10 while continuing to accrue them.

Why the September update matters

The dispute is no longer just a historical Board benchmarking example. It now combines a live long-term contract, ongoing monthly accruals, payment withholding, related-party history, an unresolved termination provision and active litigation.

CEA's latest filing states that an adverse result could leave the company bound to the existing terms for the remainder of the 20-year term. It also says the contract continues to constrain the Board's ability to terminate or restructure treasury-management arrangements while enforceability remains unresolved.

What a live stress test would need to freeze

  • Original AMA and all later amendments or modifications
  • Why the public fee moved from the 1.75% figure cited in March to the 1.4% rate disclosed in September
  • Current unpaid-fee ledger and accrual treatment
  • Asset-manager warrants and related-party history
  • Exact Section 13(a) termination mechanics
  • Current litigation docket and any ruling after August 14
  • Same-role public DAT manager comparables with normalized duration and exit rights

Product implication

CEA / 10X shows why a useful DAT benchmark is not a static fee table. The decision record must connect ORIGINAL ECONOMICS → BOARD BENCHMARK → CURRENT FEE → UNPAID ACCRUALS → TERMINATION EXPOSURE → LITIGATION STATUS.

What this case note does not say

  • It does not conclude that the agreement is fair, unfair, lawful, unlawful or unconscionable.
  • It does not endorse either party's legal position.
  • It does not treat CEA's proposed terms as a universal market rate.
  • It reports public factual and commercial terms and the status disclosed by the company.
